Understanding Parking Permits and Zones

Parking permits are a common way for cities to manage limited parking space, especially in high-demand residential and commercial areas. If you live or work in a city center, understanding permits and zones is essential to avoid fines and towing.

1. Residential Permits

These allow residents to park on their street during restricted hours. Visitors may need temporary permits.

2. Commercial Permits

Businesses may purchase permits for employee parking or loading zones.

3. Handicap Permits

Required to park in designated accessible spaces. Display the placard or license plate clearly.

4. Visitor Permits

Some neighborhoods issue temporary permits for guests. Check with your city or homeowners association.

5. Time-Limited Zones

Permits may allow extended parking in areas with short time limits for non-permit holders.

6. How to Apply

Applications are usually handled by your city's transportation or public works department. Proof of residency or ownership is required.

7. Cost and Renewal

Permits range from $20 to $300 annually, depending on the city and zone. They must be renewed periodically.
