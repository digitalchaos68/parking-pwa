The Safest Place to Park Your Car at Night

Parking at night requires extra caution. Whether you're returning late from work or leaving a movie, choosing the right spot can make a big difference in your safety and the security of your vehicle. According to the National Crime Prevention Council, over 60% of parking lot crimes occur between 6 PM and 6 AM. By following these proven strategies, you can minimize risk and stay safe.

1. Choose Well-Lit Areas

Always park under bright lights. Avoid dark corners, covered areas with poor lighting, or spots blocked by trees. Well-lit areas have up to 50% fewer break-ins. If the light above your spot is flickering or out, choose another location.

2. Stay Close to Entrances

Park near building entrances, security desks, or busy sidewalks. The more foot traffic, the safer the area. In malls and hospitals, upper levels with elevators and stairs are often safer than lower levels.

3. Look for Security Cameras

Spots under surveillance cameras are less likely to be targeted by criminals. Look for visible cameras or signs indicating the area is monitored. If possible, use ParkHere to note the camera's location relative to your car.

4. Avoid Isolated Spots

Don’t park at the far end of a lot or in areas with limited visibility. Stay where others are parking. Empty lots are prime targets for theft and vandalism.

5. Use Designated Safe Zones

Some lots have "safe walk" zones with security escorts or emergency call boxes. Airports, universities, and hospitals often offer these services — use them if available. Never hesitate to ask for help.

6. Check for Obstructions

Make sure your car isn’t blocked by other vehicles and that you have a clear path to the exit. A blocked car can trap you in a dangerous situation. Park in a way that allows you to drive forward out of the space.

7. Trust Your Instincts

If a spot feels unsafe, it probably is. Drive around and find a better location. Your intuition is a powerful safety tool — don’t ignore it.

Pro Tips for Women and Families

  • Arrive with a friend or family member when possible.
  • Keep your keys in hand and use the "key in fist" technique.
  • Use ParkHere to share your location with a trusted contact.
  • Avoid parking near large trucks or vans that block visibility.

What to Do If You Feel Unsafe

If you feel threatened, do not get in your car. Go back inside, call 911, or ask for a security escort. If already in your car, lock the doors and drive to a well-lit, populated area before calling for help.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is it safer to park in a garage or on the street? Garages are generally safer due to lighting, cameras, and foot traffic, but choose upper levels with more activity.

Should I honk my horn when leaving? A quick honk can alert others you're leaving, but don’t linger. Get in, lock up, and leave promptly.
