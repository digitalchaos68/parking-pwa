Emergency Preparedness: What to Do in a Parking Lot Incident

Parking lots can be the scene of unexpected incidents — from minor fender benders to medical emergencies. Being prepared can make all the difference in how you respond. According to the American Red Cross, over 4 million people are involved in vehicle accidents annually, and many occur in parking lots. Here’s how to stay safe and ready for anything.

1. Build an Emergency Kit

Keep a kit in your car with essentials: flashlight, first aid supplies, jumper cables, blanket, water, and non-perishable snacks. Consider adding a portable phone charger, whistle, and basic tools. Store it in the trunk or back seat for easy access.

2. Know Your Location

Use apps like ParkHere to save your exact GPS coordinates. This is crucial if you need to call for help. When speaking to 911, provide your location, building name, and nearby landmarks.

3. In Case of an Accident

Check for injuries, call 911 if needed, exchange information, and take photos of the scene and damage. If the other driver is uncooperative, note their license plate and vehicle description. Never leave the scene of an accident.

4. Medical Emergency

If someone collapses or has a medical issue, call 911 immediately. Provide clear location details and stay with the person until help arrives. If trained, perform CPR or use an AED if available. Many malls and airports have AEDs near elevators or security desks.

5. Car Trouble

If your car won't start, use jumper cables or call roadside assistance. Stay in your car if you feel unsafe. Use hazard lights and a flashlight to signal for help. If you're stranded at night, keep the dome light on to appear occupied.

6. Stay Visible

At night, turn on hazard lights and use a flashlight to signal for help. Wear reflective clothing if you must exit the vehicle. Avoid standing in traffic lanes.

7. Share Your Plans

Let someone know where you're going and when you expect to return. Use the WhatsApp reminder feature in ParkHere to automate this. If you don't check in, your contact can alert authorities.

Pro Tips for Solo Travelers

  • Park near security desks or well-lit areas.
  • Use ParkHere to share your live location with a trusted contact.
  • Keep your phone charged and emergency contacts saved.
  • Trust your instincts — if something feels off, go back inside.

Frequently Asked Questions

What should I do if my car battery dies? Use jumper cables if you have them, or call roadside assistance. Never attempt to jump-start a battery if it's cracked or leaking.

How often should I check my emergency kit? Every 3-6 months. Replace expired items like water, snacks, and first aid supplies.
