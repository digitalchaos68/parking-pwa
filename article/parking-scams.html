Recognizing and Reporting Parking Lot Scams

Parking lot scams are becoming increasingly common in cities. Scammers exploit confusion, urgency, or fear to trick drivers into paying fake fees or giving up personal information. According to the Better Business Bureau, parking-related scams have increased by 35% since 2022. Knowing how to recognize these scams can save you money and stress.

1. Fake Parking Attendants

Unofficial individuals may approach you asking for payment, often in cash. Always verify they are legitimate city or private lot employees. Look for uniforms, badges, and official signage. In cities like Los Angeles and Miami, scammers impersonate attendants to collect cash with no record.

2. Bogus Violation Notices

Scammers leave fake tickets on windshields with QR codes or phone numbers that lead to phishing sites or premium-rate calls. These are not official — always check with your city’s parking authority before paying.

3. "Damage" Extortion

A scammer may claim you damaged their car and demand immediate payment. Never pay on the spot — call the police. They often use pre-damaged vehicles to stage accidents. If you're unsure, use ParkHere to review your saved photo of the parking spot.

4. Distraction Thefts

One person distracts you (e.g., asking for directions) while an accomplice steals from your car. Always lock your doors and keep valuables out of sight. If something feels off, return to your vehicle immediately.

5. Fake Payment Kiosks

Modified or fake parking machines may steal your credit card information. Use official city meters or trusted apps like ParkHere, PayByPhone, or SpotHero. Check for tampering, loose parts, or mismatched branding.

6. How to Protect Yourself

Only pay through official channels. Check for uniforms, badges, and contact information. Use apps like ParkHere for digital receipts. Never scan QR codes from unsolicited notices. When in doubt, call local authorities.

7. Reporting a Scam

If you encounter a scam, report it to local authorities and the parking lot management. Share your experience on community forums or apps to warn others. Many cities have online portals for reporting parking fraud.

What to Do If You’re Scammed

  • Contact your bank immediately if you entered card details.
  • File a police report with photos and location details.
  • Report phishing QR codes to the FTC at reportfraud.ftc.gov.
  • Warn others via social media or neighborhood groups.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can a fake ticket lead to a real fine? No — only official city or lot notices are valid. Verify with your local parking authority.

Are all QR codes on parking tickets fake? Not all — but only scan those from trusted sources like city kiosks or apps.
