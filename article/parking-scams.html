Recognizing and Reporting Parking Lot Scams

Parking lot scams are becoming increasingly common in cities. Scammers exploit confusion, urgency, or fear to trick drivers into paying fake fees or giving up personal information. Knowing how to recognize these scams can save you money and stress.

1. Fake Parking Attendants

Unofficial individuals may approach you asking for payment, often in cash. Always verify they are legitimate city or private lot employees.

2. Bogus Violation Notices

Scammers leave fake tickets on windshields with QR codes or phone numbers that lead to phishing sites or premium-rate calls.

3. "Damage" Extortion

A scammer may claim you damaged their car and demand immediate payment. Never pay on the spot — call the police.

4. Distraction Thefts

One person distracts you (e.g., asking for directions) while an accomplice steals from your car.

5. Fake Payment Kiosks

Modified or fake parking machines may steal your credit card information. Use official city meters or apps.

6. How to Protect Yourself

Only pay through official channels. Check for uniforms, badges, and contact information. Use apps like ParkHere for digital receipts.

7. Reporting a Scam

If you encounter a scam, report it to local authorities and the parking lot management. Share your experience to warn others.
