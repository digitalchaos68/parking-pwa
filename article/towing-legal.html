The Legal Side of Towing: What You Need to Know

Having your car towed is a stressful and expensive experience. Understanding the legal rules around towing can help you avoid it and know what to do if it happens.

1. When Can a Car Be Towed?

Common reasons include parking in a no-parking zone, blocking a driveway, expired meter, or unauthorized spot in a private lot.

2. Public vs. Private Towing

Public towing is done by city authorities for violations. Private towing occurs on private property, often with fewer restrictions.

3. Towing Without a Sign

In public areas, signs are usually required. On private property, owners can often tow without warning, depending on local laws.

4. Impound Fees

Getting your car back can cost $100–$500+, including towing, storage, and administrative fees.

5. How to Find Your Car

Contact local police or the towing company. Some cities have online databases to locate impounded vehicles.

6. Your Rights

You have the right to know why your car was towed and where it’s being held. Some areas require the towing company to provide immediate notice.

7. Prevention

Always check signs, use apps like ParkHere to save your spot and photo, and never park in questionable areas.
